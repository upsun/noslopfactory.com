A model gives you an answer with a reference: an author, a title, a year, sometimes a link. The reference makes the answer feel finished. You can quote it, and someone else did the research.

But a language model writes references the same way it writes everything else: one likely word after another. A real paper and an invented paper can look the same in its output. A real paper can also be cited for a claim that it does not make.

## When a citation does not exist

In 2023, lawyers in the United States filed a brief that cited court decisions an AI chatbot had produced. Several of the decisions did not exist. The judge in Mata v. Avianca sanctioned the lawyers. The citations had case names, courts, and dates. They looked like real ones.

Invented sources are easier to catch than they used to be, because many assistants now search the web before they answer. But search adds a different problem. The link is real, but the claim attached to it can still be wrong.

## A five-minute routine

Use this routine for any claim you plan to repeat, publish, or act on:

1. Open the source. If the link fails or the title gives no search results, stop there.
2. Find the exact sentence. Ask the model for the quote and its location, then search the page for it.
3. Check that the source says what the answer says. A paper that "found a link" did not "prove a cause."
4. Check the date and the version. Documentation, prices, and laws change. An accurate old source can be wrong today.
5. Prefer the primary source. A blog post about a study is not the study.

A failed step does not prove the claim false. It proves that the citation does not support it, and that is enough to stop repeating it.

## Signs that need a second look

Some citations deserve more attention than others:

- A very precise number with no link, such as "73% of developers."
- A quote that sounds exactly like the point the answer wants to make.
- A source that you cannot find with its exact title.
- A real author attached to a paper about a topic outside their field.
- Several sources that all trace back to one article.

> A citation is a claim about a document. Check it like any other claim.

## Ask for less, check more

Ask for fewer sources and you get better ones. One primary source with a direct quote is easier to check than ten references.

The goal is not to stop using references from a model. The goal is to read them before your name is next to them.
